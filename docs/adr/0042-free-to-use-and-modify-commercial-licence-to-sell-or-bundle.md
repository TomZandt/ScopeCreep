# ScopeCreep is free to use and modify; selling, sharing or bundling needs a commercial licence

Date: 2026-11-22.

ScopeCreep was "Proprietary. All rights reserved." with no terms for anyone else. The product owner wants colourists and companies to use it freely, including on paid work, and to adapt it to how they work, while keeping the right to sell, share or bundle it for themselves. Donations and payments go through Buy Me a Coffee.

## Decision

- ScopeCreep is under the **ScopeCreep Licence 1.0** ([LICENSE.md](../../LICENSE.md)), a source-available licence governed by the law of England and Wales.
- Anyone may use it for any purpose, including commercial work, copy it within their own organisation (one legal entity), and modify it for that organisation's own use.
- Selling, distributing outside the organisation (changed or not), bundling, pre-installing for others, offering it as a service and using the name for a changed version need a commercial licence from the owner. Everyone else downloads it from the official source.
- Contributions grant the owner a licence to include them under any terms, including commercial licences, so the owner can keep licensing the whole product.
- [LICENSING.md](../../LICENSING.md) explains the licence in plain English with examples; the licence wins where they differ.
- Donations and payment are taken through Buy Me a Coffee; a donation grants no extra rights.

## Considered and rejected

- **An open-source licence (MIT, GPL).** Allows resale and redistribution, which the owner wants to keep.
- **PolyForm Noncommercial or Strict.** Forbid commercial use, the main use of a scopes app.
- **PolyForm Internal Use.** Close, but written for businesses' internal operations; a custom licence covers individuals, suite hire and contributions directly.
- **PolyForm Shield.** Allows redistribution and bundling except by competitors.
- **Free sharing of unmodified copies.** The owner chose the official download only, to keep one source of releases.

## Consequences

- A custom licence has not been tested in court; it should be reviewed by a solicitor before the first public release.
- The official source needs public contact details for licence requests while the repository is private.
- Third-party code keeps its own licence, so any shipped dependency needs its notices kept.
