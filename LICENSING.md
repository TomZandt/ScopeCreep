# Licensing in plain English

ScopeCreep is free to use, including for paid work. You can change it to suit how you work. What you can't do is sell it, share it, or package it into something else without asking first.

Think of it like a good set of workshop tools lent to you for free: use them on paying jobs, sharpen and adjust them for your own bench, but don't rebadge them and sell them, or hand them out with something you sell.

This page explains the [ScopeCreep Licence](LICENSE.md) with examples. It is a guide, not the licence: where they differ, [LICENSE.md](LICENSE.md) applies.

## In short

| You can | You can't, without a commercial licence |
|---|---|
| Use ScopeCreep for anything, including paid client work | Sell it, rent it or charge for it |
| Install it on every machine in your company | Give or post copies outside your company: point people to the [official download](https://github.com/TomZandt/ScopeCreep) instead |
| Change the code for your own or your company's use | Share or publish your changed version |
| Keep everything you make with it | Bundle it with a product, course, hardware, system image or service |
| Send improvements back to be built in | Use the ScopeCreep name for your own version, or present it as your work |

Your company means the one legal entity you work for. A sister company in the same group counts as a separate company.

## Examples

### Allowed

- **A freelance colourist** grades a paid commercial with ScopeCreep on the second monitor. Free, no need to ask.
- **A staff colourist at a broadcaster** uses ScopeCreep on air-bound programmes. The broadcaster pays nothing.
- **A post house** installs ScopeCreep on all 30 of its grading and edit suites. Its IT team may copy the installer between its own machines.
- **The same post house** changes the code to add its own house false colour scheme, and uses that build in every suite on client work. Allowed: the changed build stays inside the company.
- **A facility hires out a suite** with ScopeCreep installed, and the visiting freelancer uses it during the booking. Allowed, as long as the facility doesn't charge for ScopeCreep on its own or advertise it as something it supplies.
- **A colourist sells the graded film**, and posts before-and-after stills with the scopes in shot. Everything you make with ScopeCreep is yours.
- **A trainer teaches a grading class** with ScopeCreep, records a YouTube tutorial about it, and tells students to download it from the official page.
- **A student** uses ScopeCreep for coursework and changes the code to learn how the waveform works.
- **Someone fixes a bug** and opens a pull request so everyone gets the fix. Encouraged.

### Not allowed without a commercial licence

- **Reselling**: listing ScopeCreep, or a changed version, on a marketplace or website for a fee.
- **Passing copies around**: putting the installer on a forum, a shared community drive or a USB stick for a colleague at another company. Send them the official link instead.
- **Sharing a changed version** with anyone outside your company, including a partner company or a client, or publishing it on GitHub (a fork to prepare a pull request is fine).
- **Bundling**: including ScopeCreep in a plugin bundle, a LUT pack, a paid course download, or a "complete grading toolkit".
- **Pre-installing for others**: a hardware maker or reseller ships workstations or panels with ScopeCreep on them; a cloud provider offers rental machines with it in the image.
- **Selling it as a service**: running ScopeCreep as a remote or hosted scopes service for other people.
- **Rebranding**: renaming a changed version ("ProScope X") or taking the copyright notices out.
